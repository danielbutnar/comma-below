## Romanian Comma Below 1: Write
https://www.kaggle.com/benchmarks/tasks/danielbutnar/romanian-comma-below-1-write

Part of **Comma Below**, a benchmark on one Romanian letter pair: ș ț (s and t with a comma below,
U+0219 and U+021B, the correct Romanian letters) versus ş ţ (s and t with a cedilla, U+015F and U+0163),
a legacy encoding error that still fills the Romanian web. They look almost identical and are different
characters: search, sorting, spell-check and screen readers treat them differently.

**This task:** English prompts ask for Romanian text (translations with ASCII place names, single words, capitals, JSON, JavaScript, HTML, longer copy). Does the model write the correct letters?

**Scoring:** an item passes when the answer has zero cedilla or look-alike letters and every required word (for example *Timișoara*) appears code-point exact (case-insensitive); items without required words must contain at least one comma-below letter. Score = share of items passed. Every check is done on Unicode code points after NFC normalization. No LLM judge.

This source file is ASCII-only on purpose (Romanian letters are escapes) and verifies a SHA-256 of its
data before running, so no editor, CLI or notebook conversion can silently swap the letters under test.

## Romanian Comma Below 13: Other Wrong Letters
https://www.kaggle.com/benchmarks/tasks/danielbutnar/romanian-comma-below-13-other-wrong-letters

Part of **Comma Below**, a benchmark on one Romanian letter pair: ș ț (s and t with a comma below,
U+0219 and U+021B, the correct Romanian letters) versus ş ţ (s and t with a cedilla, U+015F and U+0163),
a legacy encoding error that still fills the Romanian web. They look almost identical and are different
characters: search, sorting, spell-check and screen readers treat them differently.

**This task:** task 8's 16 prompts where models reuse the source's words (6 news summaries and 10 questions about a document, built on 8 source texts), with the source typed five ways: (a) with the cedilla, as in task 8 (the control); (b) with s and t with a dot below (ṣ ṭ): near-identical to the right letters and real letters elsewhere (Yoruba, transliteration), but not used in Romanian; (c) as the old mojibake of Romanian web pages and subtitles (Windows-1250 bytes read as Windows-1252: Brașov becomes Braºov, țară becomes þarã), which also turns ă into ã; (d) with the cedilla and task 10's scoped rule in the system prompt; (e) with the cedilla and task 7's full rule, for a same-run comparison with (d). Every s/t letter of the source's words is scored in the answer as kept in the input's form, comma below (right), cedilla, or bare; ă is scored apart. Pre-registered predictions (written before any run; new runs on the same 16 prompts): (1) Main test: the dot below is kept in a smaller share of s/t letters than the cedilla, on the same models and prompts (paired difference, 95 % interval below zero). Kept about as often would mean any letter-like substitute gets copied; kept less often, that the familiar wrong spelling is what gets kept. (2) The old mojibake is kept less often than the cedilla; kept about as often would mean models copy whatever the input has. Romanian mojibake looks rare in training text (no tokenizer merges for it), so here 'seen in Romanian text' and 'the cedilla is special' predict the same result. (3) Repaired dot-below and mojibake letters become comma-below letters, not the cedilla, in at least 90 % of cases. (4) Both rules give at least 90 % clean answers, and the scoped rule is within 5 points of the full rule. (5) Replication of task 8: in cedilla answers that keep every cedilla of the input, at least 80 % of the s/t words the model adds itself (not in the source and not inflections of source words: no shared start of 5 letters) have the cedilla; in answers that keep only some, at most 30 %. Answers that comment on the encoding are flagged and reported with and without; answers with none of the source's s/t words count as no data.

**Scoring:** an answer passes when it has no cedilla, dot-below, mojibake (º ª þ Þ) or look-alike (ã ǎ) letters and at least one comma-below letter. Score = pass rate on the three wrong-letter inputs without a rule (a, b, c). The results file records, for every answer, each s/t and ă letter of the source's words as written (slot counts), the word-level forms as in task 8, the wrong-form words and which of them were copied from the input, and the words with which an answer comments on the encoding. Every check is done on Unicode code points after NFC normalization. No LLM judge.

This source file is ASCII-only on purpose (Romanian letters are escapes) and verifies a SHA-256 of its
data before running, so no editor, CLI or notebook conversion can silently swap the letters under test.
